The admin portal — how to use it

The CAPI Console holds respondent-level survey data, so every person who opens it has a named account and a role that decides what they can reach. This guide covers signing in, the screens in the admin portal, and the handful of tasks you will actually do: adding someone, removing someone, resetting a password, and finding out who looked at what.

Everyone reads the first section. The sign-in and password rules apply to all console accounts, not just administrators. The rest of the guide is for the two roles that can administer accounts.

Signing in

The console sign-in page is capi.asiansocial.org/docs/idp/login. Old links still work — they redirect here — so a bookmark to the previous page is fine.

Your first sign-in will ask you to change your password. That is expected, not a fault. Sign in with the console password you already use, and you will be taken straight to a page to set a new one. You cannot reach anything else until you do.

A new password must:

  • be at least 12 characters — length matters more than punctuation, so a short phrase you can remember beats a mangled word;
  • not contain your username;
  • not be one of the well-known setup passwords, or contain capi, aspsi or 123456.

Changing your password later, by choice, additionally asks for your current password — and signs out every other device you are signed in on.

RuleWhat it means for you
Signed out after 60 minutes idleLeave the tab alone for an hour and you sign in again.
Signed out after 12 hours regardlessA working day is one sign-in.
8 wrong passwords locks the account for 15 minutesWait it out, or ask an administrator to clear it.
20 failures from one network blocks that network for 15 minutesOnly comes up if something is repeatedly guessing.

None of this affects tablet syncing. CSEntry uses its own sync login and never asks for a console password. If the console is unavailable, fieldwork carries on.

Finding your way around

The admin portal is at capi.asiansocial.org/docs/admin/. The sidebar is in four groups:

GroupScreensWhat it governs
Access Users & access · Roles & permissions · Active sessions · Audit trail Who may open the console and what they may see.
Console Activities · Alerting · Assignment plan How the console behaves. Nothing here decides access.
You My account Your own password, roles and sessions.
Elsewhere Overview · Sync dashboard · Data room · Sign out Links out of the portal.
You only see what you can use. Screens your account cannot open are hidden from the sidebar. If a whole group disappears, that is why — not a fault.

The five roles

A person is given a role; the role carries permissions. You assign roles on the Users screen; you change what a role means on the Roles screen.

RoleCan reachTypically
Owner Everything, including editing what each role is allowed to do. Carl. Keep the number of owners very small.
Programme Admin Everything except editing role permissions — including adding and removing accounts. ASPSI staff who onboard and offboard the field team.
Analyst Monitoring, individual cases, tabulations, and the data room (bulk export). Someone doing analysis on the collected data.
Field Supervisor Monitoring, individual cases and tabulations. No bulk export. Supervisors checking their team’s work. Reading one case for QC is not the same act as taking the whole dataset.
Client Viewer Monitoring and tabulations only. No respondent-level detail. DOH, when they should see progress and tables but not individual answers.

Users & access

The list shows every console account. The columns worth knowing:

ColumnReading it
Statusactive · setup password (has not chosen their own yet) · locked out (too many wrong attempts) · disabled.
SessionsHow many devices are signed in as that account right now. A dot means none.
HashHow the password is stored. apr1 means the account is still on the password it was migrated with; it upgrades itself the next time they sign in. Nothing to do.
duplicate badgeAnother account differs from this one only by an underscore versus a hyphen (se_001 and se-001). Compare their sign-in history before disabling either — they may be two different people.

Click a username to open it. Everything below happens on that page.

Adding someone

  1. Users & access → Add account.
  2. Username in lowercase — letters, digits, dot, underscore or hyphen.
  3. Tick the role. If in doubt, pick the narrower one; widening later is one click.
  4. Save. A temporary password appears once.
Copy the temporary password before you close the dialog. The server keeps only a scrambled form of it, so it genuinely cannot be shown again. If it is lost, reset the password — that is quicker than looking for it.

Give it to the person directly, not in a group chat. They will be made to change it the first time they sign in.

Removing someone

Open the account and choose Disable account. You will be asked to type the username to confirm, and told how many sessions it will end. Disabling takes effect immediately — any device signed in as that account is signed out on its next click.

There is no delete, deliberately. Removing the account outright would also remove the record of what it did. Disabling blocks sign-in and keeps the history. To bring someone back, open the account and re-enable it.

Some things are refused on purpose:

  • You cannot disable your own account, or change your own roles. Ask another administrator.
  • The last remaining owner cannot be disabled or demoted.
  • Two break-glass accounts (aspsi, marriz_admin) cannot be disabled here — that is what makes them a way back in.
  • Only an owner can grant the owner role, or change an owner’s roles.

Passwords and sessions

You want to…Do thisWhat happens
Give someone a new password Open the account → Reset password A new temporary password is shown once; their old one stops working and every session ends.
Clear a lockout Open the account → Clear lockout They can try again straight away. Their password is unchanged.
Sign someone out everywhere Open the account → Sign out everywhere All their sessions end. Their password still works, so they can sign back in.
End one suspicious session Active sessions → End session Only that one device is signed out.

Active sessions lists everyone signed in right now, with the client and IP address. Your own session is marked and cannot be ended from there — use Sign out in the sidebar instead.

Roles & permissions

This screen is the grid of five roles against seven permissions. Everyone who administers accounts can read it; only an owner can change it.

Changing a role affects every person who holds it, at once. Saving signs out everyone with that role, so a permission you remove stops applying immediately rather than at their next sign-in. The hatched cells on the owner row cannot be un-ticked — removing them would leave nobody able to administer the console.

Moving one person between roles is the Users screen, not this one. Come here only when the meaning of a role should change.

Audit trail

Every sign-in, failed sign-in, permission refusal, account change and — since the console gate moved — every read of respondent data is recorded with who, when and from which address.

  • Filter by actor, action, target or date. Typing user. in the action box selects the whole family of account changes.
  • Click an actor to filter to just them.
  • Export CSV gives you exactly the rows your filters are showing — not the whole table.

The trail is append-only. Nothing in the portal can edit or delete a row, including this screen, and that is enforced by the database rather than by good manners.

When something goes wrong

SymptomWhat it meansWhat to do
“Set a new password before using the admin tools” The account is still on its setup password. Change it. Nothing else works until you do.
“Too many attempts. Try again in N minutes.” Eight wrong passwords. Wait, or ask an administrator to clear the lockout.
Someone forgot their password Normal. Reset it for them and hand over the temporary one directly.
“Not permitted” Signed in, but the role does not carry that permission. Check the role on the Users screen. Widen only if it should be widened.
Sent back to sign-in repeatedly The session expired, or an administrator ended it. Sign in again. If it repeats immediately, the account may have been disabled.
“Console temporarily unavailable” The sign-in service is down. Nothing is lost. Wait a minute and retry. Tablet syncing is unaffected — tell the field team to carry on.
A screen says a change failed It failed. The portal never reports success for something it did not save. Reload and try again. If it mentions a request id, quote it when reporting.
Nobody can get in at all? That is what the break-glass route is for, and it needs the server key rather than a console password. It is written up in the deployment runbook — contact Carl.

Two habits worth keeping

  • Offboard the same day. Disabling takes effect immediately and keeps the person’s history — there is no reason to leave it until the end of the week.
  • Give the narrowest role that works. Field Supervisor can review individual cases without being able to export the whole dataset; that difference is the point of having five roles rather than one.
